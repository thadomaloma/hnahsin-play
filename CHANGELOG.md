# What's new

Each version of the Hnahsin web games. The newest is at the top.
Versions follow `major.minor.patch`: a new game or a big change is a new
minor version, a fix is a new patch version.

## 1.68.4 (13 November 2026)

- Aizawl Ride's taxis look like the real Alto 800: the headlamps set into
  the front below the bonnet, full-width bumpers with round ends, rounder
  corners, dark rain visors over the side windows, and mud flaps behind the
  wheels.
- Every car on the road is rounder where its body turns (its shoulders, roof
  and sills), not cut in flat facets.

## 1.68.3 (13 November 2026)

- Aizawl Ride's scooter sounds like a scooter: each firing a kick of gas
  down a real length of exhaust pipe and through the silencer, the engine's
  soft clatter under it, a rougher beat at idle, and a little of the sound
  coming back off the hillside and the walls round you. The belt's whirr is
  fainter, and the brakes only squeal for a moment as they bite.

## 1.68.2 (13 November 2026)

- Aizawl Ride's Moped looks like the real thing: a round rear body under the
  seat, sweeping back over the wheel to the tail lamp; a cream leg shield
  curving round your legs, with a rounded cover over the steering head; and
  a round front mudguard that turns with the handlebar. Its frame, basket,
  carrier and engine stay as they were.

## 1.68.1 (13 November 2026)

- Aizawl Ride's Sport and Electric scooters have real round bodies too.
  The Sport is a sleek wedge rising to its tail, with dark lower panels, a
  sharp front cowl holding twin LED lamps, an apron round your legs, a
  tunnel between your feet and a small mudguard close over the front tyre.
  The Electric is one smooth body with a tall, smooth front holding its dark
  glass panel, LED strip and lamp, and its screen on a rounded handlebar
  cover.

## 1.68.0 (13 November 2026)

- Aizawl Ride's delivery scooters have real round bodies. The Everyday and
  the Classic are shaped as a scooter is pressed from steel: the body round
  and swelling over the back wheel, narrowing up to the seat; the front
  shield curving back round your legs; the mudguard rounded over the front
  wheel, its sides coming down past the tyre; a rounded handlebar cover with
  the lamp in its chrome ring; a rounded seat. Your colours and puan patterns
  stay as you chose them.
- The women's puan is wrapped as a puan is: snug at the waist, following the
  hips and falling straight to the middle of the calf, with a straight hem
  all round. It is woven as one is: a broad stripe and two fine lines in her
  colour over and over on a dark ground, a border along the hem, soft folds
  and the edge of the wrap down the front.

## 1.67.4 (13 November 2026)

- The people in Aizawl Ride look more at ease: their hands rest a little
  curled, not spread flat, and standing they keep their arms by their sides.
- As you ride close by, some of them turn their heads to watch you go.
- The riders in the traffic hold their handlebars with their hands closed
  round the grips.

## 1.67.3 (13 November 2026)

- The games open faster on your phone. An update no longer fetches
  everything again: the game engines, the Hnahsin app, Aizawl Ride's people,
  far city and moped stay on your phone until they really change. When the
  network is slow, a game opens from your phone after three seconds instead
  of waiting.
- Aizawl Ride is lighter on your phone: water tanks only on the buildings
  near enough to see, and full bodies only for the people near you.
- Files no game used are gone.

### Aizawl Ride: new colours, real riders

## 1.60.0 (12 November 2026)

### Aizawl Ride: a scooter that rides like one

- The hills slow you down, as they would a loaded delivery scooter: about
  36 km/h up a climb of 1 in 10, 27 up 1 in 7, 22 up 1 in 5, and 14 to 16 km/h
  up Aizawl's steepest streets, but it never stalls. Down a steep hill the rider
  brakes a little and goes slower than on the flat.
- You cruise at 40 km/h, up to 58 on a long ride (it was up to 65), and take
  about five seconds to get there from a standstill.
- The view widens a little as you go faster, and the engine works harder on a
  climb, even slowly.
- Changing lanes is smooth: the scooter leans into the move and steers across
  in one sweep. Before, the view lagged behind it, so it swung past its lane and
  slid back.

## 1.58.0 (6 November 2026)

### Home page: hnahsin as it really grows

- The cosmos leaves are drawn as cosmos leaves are: a gently arched midrib with
  long thread-like segments in near-opposite pairs, curving toward the tip, the
  lower ones split again into finer threads. They are longer too, near as long
  as the flower is wide.
- The petals blowing across the top of the page are cosmos petals, not ovals:
  narrow at the base, widening to a broad tip with three small teeth, the colour
  deeper at the base and fine lines running along them. They still tumble as
  they drift.
- The petals that scatter when you tap a flower have the same shape.

## 1.57.0 (6 November 2026)

### Aizawl Ride: busier roads, fewer road works, a real bus stop

- Twice as much traffic both ways, all of it more cars, taxis, SUVs,
  scooters and motorbikes; buses and trucks come as often as before. The
  queues are twice as long, 12 to 23 vehicles.
- Road works are half as common as they were; potholes, parked cars, trucks
  unloading and buses make up the rest.
- A bus stops at a proper bus stop: a bay marked in yellow on the road, and on
  the footpath a shelter with a blue roof, posters on its back, a wooden bench
  and a blue BUS STOP sign. People wait at its front, and one or two sit on the
  bench. No one under its roof needs an umbrella.

### Aizawl Ride: people walking in the flat (2D) view

- The smoother walk is back: each foot lifts and comes forward in turn, the
  arms swing against the legs, and the body rises a little at each step.
- People standing still breathe, sway, shift their weight from foot to foot and
  blink now and then, each in their own time.

## 1.56.0 (6 November 2026)

### Aizawl Ride: road works at work

- The road works are being built as you pass. Two workers dig the trench,
  pushing the shovel in, prising the earth up and throwing it on the heap; the
  lumps fly and land in a little dust. One rakes out new tar, and a small road
  roller with its driver rolls it to and fro, its lamp turning and its exhaust
  puffing. A drum of tar heats over a wood fire, its smoke drifting off the
  road. The man with the red flag waves it for the traffic to come past.
- At a pothole, a worker pounds the gravel down with a rammer, a puff of dust
  each time it lands.
- The workers and the roller's driver have real bodies, like the people on the
  footpaths, posed for their work: they bend over the shovel, lean into the
  rake and sit at the roller's wheel, with both hands on their tools. They wear
  hard hats, gloves and vests with shining bands. On the lowest picture quality,
  and until the bodies have loaded, they are drawn simply.

### Aizawl Ride: words and pictures

- Each Mizo word you meet on the road is said aloud, and the words you meet and

- Today's parcel shows a picture of what you carry (cucumbers, cabbages, a

## 1.54.0 (4 November 2026)

### Aizawl Ride: busy footpaths, families, dogs and umbrellas

- About twice as many people walk the footpaths, and the footpaths ahead are
  full from the start of a ride, not only as people come into view.
- Some walk together: a parent with a child, two children together (in their
  school uniforms), or someone walking their dog on a red lead. The dogs
  trot along beside, brown, black, white, tan or grey, their tails up.
- In the rain everyone on foot has an umbrella up: most of them black, as in
  Aizawl, some blue, red, purple, green, yellow or pink. The road workers and
  the porters, whose hands are full, do not.
- Children are smaller in the 2D view too.

### Aizawl Ride: fixes to the last days' changes

- The road workers, the porters and some taxi passengers had no skin colour
  and showed pale white; they have their own now.
- Parked vehicles with their hazard lights on no longer have their headlamps
  and beams on at night.
- Nothing parks across a side road on the left any more (no car, truck or bus
  stopped in a junction's opening); road works go there instead.
- A long queue before a junction with a main road reached through the
  junction; it now ends 5 m before it.
- The zebra crossing before a junction was often moved away, as it fell in the
  junction's opening; it is a little further back now, so more junctions have
  one.
- A sign no longer stands in a side road.
- People at a bus stop, and porters, check for a footpath where they stand.
- Busy streets stay smooth: only the 16 nearest people have full bodies (those
  further off are drawn simply), and there is room for many more people and
  riders, so none lose an arm, a leg or a head.

## 1.53.0 (4 November 2026)

### Aizawl Ride: junctions, busier streets, children and more two-wheelers

- Where another road meets yours, as it does on the real map (from
  OpenStreetMap: main roads and streets, up to about six to a kilometre), there is a
  junction now. The kerb and footpath open, and the side road goes off up or
  down the hillside, no steeper than 1 in 10, with a white line where it meets
  your road and dashes up its middle. Nothing is built on it: no houses, trees
  or poles.
- Queues wait just before the junctions with main roads, and they are longer
  (6 to 11 vehicles). There is a zebra crossing just before many junctions,
  with up to 7 crossings on a road.
- People stand and walk at the corners of each junction. There are more people
  walking on the footpaths too, and children among them, in their school
  uniforms with their school bags.
- There is more traffic both ways, and more than half of it is two-wheelers:
  scooters, motorbikes and sports bikes.

## 1.52.0 (4 November 2026)

## 1.51.0 (3 November 2026)

### Aizawl Ride: roadblocks as Aizawl has them

- The logs, rocks and barriers in the road are gone. What blocks a lane now
  is what blocks one in Aizawl:
  - a car parked by the kerb, its hazard lights flashing;
  - a goods truck unloading, its sacks set down on the road behind it;
  - an Aizawl City Bus at its stop;
  - road works: barricades at each end with flashing lamps, cones along the

  - a pothole being mended, with cones round it and rainwater in it;
  - a landslide (leimin) of mud and rocks across the lane, more of them in the

- Parked vehicles, trucks and buses stand by the kerb, in the left lane.
  Each roadblock is as long as it is drawn, so a bus or a truck takes longer

- The first time each kind is ahead, its Mizo word is shown: motor ding,

  kawng siam are in the end-of-ride word questions too.

## 1.50.0 (3 November 2026)

### Aizawl Ride: no branches over the road, and betel-nut palms

- Some trees reached out over the road: 543 of them on the 148 roads, nearly
  all bamboo clumps, whose canes lean out up to 9 m, and a few broad-leaved
  trees. A tree was kept 2.2 m clear of the road whatever its size. Now each
  kind of tree is measured, branches and leaves too, and all of it is kept
  clear of every stretch of road (a tree on a bank well above the road may
  still reach out high over it). Checked on every road: none is over the road
  now, and there are about as many trees as before.
- A bamboo clump leans one way now, out over the slope, away from the road.
- Betel-nut palms (kuhva), as by so many Mizo houses, grow by the road too: a
  tall, thin, ringed trunk with a crown of fronds.

## 1.49.0 (3 November 2026)

### Aizawl Ride: land all round, no more holes past the hillside

- Past the ground beside the road (about 60 m out) there was nothing, so in
  about one view in six a patch of bare haze showed below the hills, like a
  hole in the ground: across a valley, in a bend, and at the start and end of
  a ride. Now the land carries on to about 340 m from the road. Its hills
  rise or fall as the ground beside the road does, down to a valley's floor
  and up again on the far side towards the next ridge. Where the road has
  many houses, it is a soft patchwork of walls and roofs, as Aizawl's
  hillsides look from across a valley.
- That far land is in a softer haze than the near ground, as the far hills
  are, so it can always be seen.
- Checked on every road (all 148, every 10 m, from where you ride): the
  view down to the valley floor below the world is gone everywhere, except
  now and then at the far edge, about 1 km away, where the haze hides it.

## 1.48.0 (3 November 2026)

### Aizawl Ride: a way back on every road of the Aizawl map

- Every delivery on the Aizawl map now has its way back: 31 new roads, so
  there are 146 in all. Before, 31 roads went only one way (from Zarkawt to
  Dinthar, say, but not from Dinthar back).
- Dinthar and Tuikual North, by the middle of town, could be reached only
  the long way round through Luangmual, so they needed 40,000 and 46,000
  points. They are now a short ride from Dawrpui, Zarkawt and Chhinga, and
  open at 5,000 and 13,000 points.
- The points each veng needs are now fixed, so no veng that is open now
  closes.
- Today's missions, the medals and the badges were checked: each can be
  done, and each counts what it says.

## 1.47.0 (3 November 2026)

### Aizawl Ride: night lights that look real, trucks, and wheels that turn

- Trucks are on the road now: a goods truck with its cab in yellow, blue,

## 1.7.0 (25 October 2026)

### New in the Playground: Word quests

- **Word dungeon.** Rova fights monsters with Mizo names: sazu (rat), bak
  (bat), rul (snake) and more. Make a word from 16 letter tiles to hit the
  monster. Long words and rare letters hit harder, and a gold tile is a
  critical hit. Each floor ends with a boss with a trick: awle (crocodile)
  stops short words, saphai (python) heals, sakei (tiger) curses tiles.
  Between fights, buy charms in the shop.
- **Falling letters.** Drag across touching letters to make words. The tiles
  vanish and the ones above fall. A new row comes up every third word; if a
  column reaches the top, the game is over. Stones and bombs make it harder.
- AW, CH and NG are one tile each, as in the Mizo alphabet. Words are checked
  against 5,627 Mizo words, and the meaning shows when we know it.
- The Playground has 31 games now, and the site 41.
